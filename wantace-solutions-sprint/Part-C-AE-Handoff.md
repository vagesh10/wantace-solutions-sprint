# Part C — Account Executive Handoff

## Account: Vardhan Steelware

### Stakeholders
- **Suresh — owner / sponsor:** Needs visibility into where orders are stuck; likely a key decision-maker. Confirm the final purchasing and approval authority.
- **Kunal — order coordinator:** Maintains or updates the order sheet and advocates for integrations and AI features.
- **Ramesh — dispatch:** Tracks dispatch activity in a diary and holds operational status information.
- **Munshi ji — accounts:** Handles account/payment records and should validate how holds and returns are reflected in Tally.
- **Polishing contractors:** Four to five external contractors are involved; contractor access should not be assumed necessary for Phase 1.

### Pain and evidence in the supplied case pack
- Suresh says he cannot tell where goods are stuck and must call Ramesh, whose diary holds key information.
- A previous ERP costing ₹1.2 lakh was used for only three months, suggesting user adoption and workflow fit are material risks.
- The supplied sheet includes possible repeated orders, a missing customer name, a missing rate, payment holds, partial dispatches, a return, and an outsourced polishing quantity discrepancy.
- Treat repeated-looking rows as possible duplicates until source messages confirm they are duplicates.

### Requests versus underlying need
- **Requested by Kunal:** Modern system, marketplace integrations, and AI sales prediction.
- **Most immediate need indicated by the evidence:** A shared, reliable order-status view with clear ownership, blockers, due dates, partial dispatch balance, and polishing send/receive reconciliation. The case states online sales are under 5% of the business, so advanced online integrations should be validated before prioritization.

### Likely objections and response
- **“We already tried an ERP and staff stopped using it.”** Start with a small pilot, a few mandatory fields, phone-friendly updates, and feedback from Ramesh and Munshi ji before expanding.
- **“We need this before Diwali.”** Confirm the exact date and Phase 1 must-haves; do not promise a delivery date before feasibility is validated.
- **“We want AI and integrations.”** Acknowledge the request, then agree how to measure basic visibility and data quality first. Revisit advanced features once the underlying records are reliable.

### Next step
Hold a workflow walkthrough with Suresh, Kunal, Ramesh, and Munshi ji. Validate order identity, stages, quantities/units, payment-hold authority, returns, and polishing reconciliation against source records. Then propose a small pilot with success criteria and a delivery estimate only after technical and user validation.
